\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Synthetic, small-scale benchmark.} The groups are compact, low-dimensional and observed through a mild
nonlinear map. We make no claim about images, physical trajectories or large pretrained models.

\textbf{No identifiability.} Closure did not prevent wrong closed algebras. Action inference is only as good as
the recovered structure. Correct-closed SO(3) runs have moderate action disagreement with the truth
($0.36$--$0.43$), while chiral and non-closed runs are no better than the identity ($\approx1$; Appendix,
Table~\ref{tab:structure}).

Our latent alignment is orthogonal Procrustes, so a model whose encoder is far from an isometry of the true state
space is scored as incorrect even if its algebra is conjugate to the truth (Proposition~\ref{prop:classify}(a)
guarantees such conjugacy for closed $T^2$ runs). The $T^2$ failures are of this kind. They are \emph{seed-level}
fitting failures: the same seeds fail under every method (102, 115 and 118 under all four, 109, 116 and 119 under
all but the final BracketNet), all with high transport error.

\textbf{Protocol choices.} The budget and $\lambda$ were selected on five development seeds. The $\lambda$ rule
needed an amendment, committed before any fresh run, because no candidate met its transport constraint; the
selected $\lambda=\NLambda$ trades some development transport for structure. The structural thresholds are
conventions fixed in advance; different thresholds change the category counts but not the continuous metrics.

\textbf{Scope of comparison.} We compare controlled objective ablations under identical networks. We did not
reimplement LieGAN, LaLiGAN or Homomorphism Autoencoders: they use different supervision signals (distributional
invariance, or transitions with known group elements), so a fair comparison would require redesigning the
benchmark. The theory is local: Proposition~\ref{prop:bch} covers only small group elements, and the generator
count $K$ is assumed known.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Explicit Lie-closure regularization makes the transformation structure learned by independent models more
algebraically consistent and more comparable. For a non-abelian group it closes spans that transition fitting
leaves open, roughly halves the distance to the true algebra, and reduces cross-seed disagreement, all without a
detectable transport cost. It does not make that structure identifiable: closed but wrong (chiral) algebras are
common, and agreement between models partly reflects agreement on a discrete set of classes, including wrong ones.
Transferring transformations between models works when both recovered the correct structure, and largely
fails otherwise. For representation alignment, the
lesson is that comparing models requires comparing \emph{what they do}, the algebra acting on the
representation, and not only \emph{where they embed}.
