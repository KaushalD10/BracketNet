\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
When two networks are trained on the same data, do they learn the same thing? Representation-similarity measures
answer this for \emph{embeddings}: CKA, Procrustes and shape metrics compare point clouds up to a class of
transformations \citep{kornblith2019similarity,williams2021generalized}. For models that learn how data
\emph{transform}, a second question matters: does the learned transformation structure agree? Here that structure
is a set of latent generators whose exponentials act on the representation. Two models can embed data similarly
yet represent transformations through incompatible algebras, in which case actions inferred by one model are
meaningless in the other.

Transition-based symmetry learners \citep{park2022learning,keurti2023homomorphism,yang2024latent} fit local
transformations, but nothing in a transition loss requires the learned generators $A_1,\dots,A_K$ to span a Lie
algebra, i.e.\ $[A_i,A_j]\in\spn\{A_k\}$. Without closure, the nominal $K$-parameter family is not locally closed
under composition, and its ``structure constants'' are not defined. Closure is therefore a natural candidate for a
\emph{structural} inductive bias: it restricts each model to a subalgebra of $\so(d)$. In the low-dimensional settings
we study, these fall into a few conjugacy classes (Proposition~\ref{prop:classify}), which can be compared across
models; in general the classification is richer, e.g.\ one-dimensional subalgebras of a torus form a continuum.

We test this idea carefully and report what holds and what does not. We fixed every choice on development seeds,
with selection rules committed before the runs, and evaluated once on fresh seeds with a pre-registered primary
hypothesis family. Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A precise account of what a closure loss can guarantee} (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}). We give its exact
behaviour under changes of generator coordinates (invariant only under orthogonal changes; its zero set is
$GL(K)$-invariant), and a rigorous bound showing that how far products of near-identity elements leave the learned
span vanishes with the closure defect. We also classify the closed subalgebras in our benchmarks: for SO(3) inside
$\so(4)$, closure admits two incorrect, chiral classes.
\item \textbf{A structural evaluation} that separates closure, ground-truth recovery, cross-model agreement and
action accuracy, together with \textbf{cross-model transformation transfer}: actions inferred by one model are
applied in another model's aligned latent space.
\item \textbf{Evidence on \NNseeds{} fresh seeds} (Secs.~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:ablations}). Closure
regularization substantially improves algebraic consistency for SO(3) at no detectable transport cost, but turns
continuous disagreement between models into discrete disagreement among closed classes, including frequent wrong
ones. Transfer quality tracks structural correctness, not the training objective.
\end{itemize}
