\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Synthetic scope.} Both benchmarks, including the rendered one, are synthetic, low-dimensional and
compact. We make no claim about natural images, physical systems or large models.
\item \textbf{Known $K$, orthogonal actions.} Closure cannot discover the dimension and presupposes compact
(orthogonal) actions.
\item \textbf{No identifiability.} Wrong closed algebras are frequent for SO(3), and correct algebras still have
irreducible action ambiguity (Prop.~\ref{prop:endpoint}c). Our ground-truth comparisons use orthogonal Procrustes, so
they assume a near-isometric encoder; the $T^2$ failures are seed-level fitting failures shared by all methods.
\item \textbf{Limited baselines.} We compare controlled objective variants and an action-supervised oracle; we do not
reimplement published methods with different supervision.
\item \textbf{Statistics and protocol.} We use 20 seeds; effects outside SO(3) are small or concentrated in few
seeds. No transport improvement is established. The closure-weight rule needed an amendment, committed before any fresh
run. One pre-registered prediction (structure beats CKA at predicting transfer failure) was not supported.
\item \textbf{Theory is local or benchmark-specific.} The escape bound covers only small actions, the classification is
specific to $\so(4)$ and $\so(5)$, and Prop.~\ref{prop:endpoint} characterizes the loss at the ground-truth embedding,
not the optimization path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Penalizing Lie closure changes \emph{how} independently trained transformation models disagree. Continuous scatter
over generator spans becomes a choice among a few closed subalgebras. For SO(3) this raises algebraic agreement
between models, and it lets actions transfer between models that made the same choice. But the choice is often wrong,
for a reason we can state precisely: actions inferred from endpoints cannot see rotations about the axis through a
point, and a chiral algebra hides that blind spot by fitting transitions and compositions exactly. For representation
alignment, the lesson is that agreement must be measured at the level of transformations as well as embeddings, and
that agreement at either level is not evidence of correctness.
