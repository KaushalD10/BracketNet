# Mathematical verification

This file covers every mathematical statement in the final manuscript (`paper/bracketnet_final.tex`) and in the
original submission. Each item gives a verdict, the corrected statement, a proof or proof sketch with standard
references, and the automated check.

* `tests/test_math.py`: 19 tests, carried over from the first audit.
* `tests/test_math_verification.py`: 20 tests, written for this verification.

"Verified" below means proved here **and** checked numerically. "Numerically checked" means tested but not proved
beyond the sketch given. No external reviewer has checked these proofs.

Notation:
* A_1, …, A_K ∈ so(d), and B ∈ R^{d²×K} holds their vectorizations as columns.
* G = BᵀB, and 𝔞 = span(A).
* P_ε = B(G + εI)⁻¹Bᵀ. The exact orthogonal projector onto 𝔞 is P₀ (defined with the pseudo-inverse when G is singular).
* N = I − P₀.
* L_close(A; ε) = K⁻² Σ_{i,j} ‖(I − P_ε) vec[A_i, A_j]‖².

---

## 1. Basis invariance of the closure loss: original claim **false**, corrected

**Original:** "Because P_B depends only on the span, Eq. (7) is invariant to any invertible change of generator coordinates."

**Corrected statement (verified, T1/T2).**

**(a) Exact projector, ε = 0.** P₀ depends only on 𝔞. Under B ↦ BM with M ∈ GL(K), the commutators transform as
[A'_i, A'_j] = Σ_{k,l} M_{ki} M_{lj} [A_k, A_l]. Hence, with R = N·C the d²×K² residual matrix,

    L_close(BM; 0) = K⁻² ‖R (M ⊗ M)‖_F²,   σ_min(M)⁴ L_close(B; 0) ≤ L_close(BM; 0) ≤ σ_max(M)⁴ L_close(B; 0).

Consequences:
* L_close is invariant exactly under orthogonal M.
* It scales as s⁴ under M = sI.
* Its zero set {L_close = 0} = {𝔞 is a subalgebra} is GL(K)-invariant.

Proof: substitute and use that P₀(BM) = P₀(B). The bounds follow from
σ_min(M ⊗ M) = σ_min(M)² and σ_max(M ⊗ M) = σ_max(M)².
Tests: `test_T1_gl_transformation_law`, `test_T1_orthogonal_invariance_and_zero_set`.

**(b) Regularized projector, ε > 0.** P_ε(BM) = B(G + ε M⁻ᵀM⁻¹)⁻¹Bᵀ. So P_ε is invariant under orthogonal M and,
for general M, is not even a function of the span. Test: `test_T2_regularized_projector_law`.

For the generators the method actually uses (each ‖A_k‖_F = √2, so diag G = 2, and G is near 2I under the Gram
penalty), ε = 10⁻⁶ changes L_close by a relative O(ε). Test: `test_T2_eps_effect_is_negligible_for_normalized_bases`.
We report ε = 0 values as `closure_residual_exact` in every run file.

**(c) A genuinely span-only objective exists.** Use U = √2·B G^{-1/2}, a √2-scaled orthonormal basis of 𝔞, and
evaluate L_close(U; 0). This is a function of 𝔞 alone, hence GL(K)-invariant. Tests:
`test_T1_whitened_closure_is_span_only`, plus a torch implementation check.

Its gradient involves G^{-1/2} and becomes ill-conditioned as G approaches singularity. Under the Gram penalty and
√2 normalization, G stayed well-conditioned in every run (see `gram_condition` in the run files).

Whether to switch objectives was decided on development seeds by a pre-registered rule (§9). The evaluated method
keeps Eq. (7); the paper states its invariance exactly as in (a)–(b), and the whitened variant appears only as an
ablation and a diagnostic.

## 2. Generator normalization and Gram penalty: verified; original pilot explanation **removed**

Each A_k is antisymmetrized and scaled to ‖A_k‖_F = √2 on every forward pass. Hence:
* the normalization is idempotent, so diag G = 2;
* L_basis(target c) = ‖G − cI‖_F² = Σ_{i≠j} G_ij² + K(2 − c)².

The penalty therefore acts only through the off-diagonal Gram entries, and **every target c gives the same gradient**
(test `test_T3_normalization_and_gram`, plus the previous gradient test). Empirically, swapping the target from
2I to I changed closure by at most 4 × 10⁻⁸ (first-round ablation).

The original manuscript's explanation for a failed pilot ("the target I penalized the true normalized basis") cannot
hold for an implementation that normalizes before the penalty, and no other implementation exists. The final
manuscript drops it.

## 3. Proposition 1 (exact closure integrates): **correct as stated**

If A_1, …, A_K are linearly independent and [A_i, A_j] ∈ 𝔞 for all i, j, then 𝔞 is a K-dimensional Lie subalgebra
of so(d). By the Lie subgroup theorem (Hall 2015, Thm 5.20), there is a unique connected Lie subgroup H ⊆ SO(d)
with Lie algebra 𝔞, generated by exp(𝔞). H is immersed, and not necessarily closed: an irrational line in a torus is
the standard example.

The commutator is bilinear and antisymmetric and satisfies Jacobi in any associative algebra, so "closure ⇒
subalgebra" needs only bilinearity. Tests: `test_true_algebras_close`, `test_jacobi_exact_for_closed_algebras`.

## 4. Lemma 1 (closure loss controls the uniform escape constant): **verified**, general form

Define the uniform escape constant δ(𝔞) = sup_{X, Y ∈ 𝔞 ∖ 0} ‖N[X, Y]‖_F / (‖X‖_F ‖Y‖_F).

**Lemma.** δ(𝔞) ≤ K √(L_close(A; 0)) / λ_min(G).

**Proof.** Write X = Σ x_i A_i and Y = Σ y_j A_j, and let r_ij = N[A_i, A_j]. Then N[X, Y] = Σ x_i y_j r_ij.
* By Cauchy–Schwarz over the K² index pairs, ‖N[X, Y]‖ ≤ ‖x‖ ‖y‖ (Σ‖r_ij‖²)^{1/2} = ‖x‖ ‖y‖ K √L_close.
* Since ‖X‖² = xᵀGx ≥ λ_min(G) ‖x‖² (and the same for Y), ‖x‖ ‖y‖ ≤ ‖X‖ ‖Y‖ / λ_min(G).

With G = 2I this reduces to δ ≤ (K/2) √L_close, the form in the earlier draft. ∎

Test: `test_T4_lemma1_delta_bound` compares the bound against δ computed by multi-start alternating maximization
over unit pairs, on non-normalized and non-orthogonal bases at three perturbation sizes. Every run records
`delta_bound`.

## 5. Proposition 2 (approximate closure controls BCH escape): **original true but loose; corrected and proved**

**Original:** dist(log(e^X e^Y), 𝔞) ≤ C₁ δ r² + C₂ r³. Its cubic term does not vanish under exact closure, so it
is weaker than Proposition 1 implies, and the proof was a sketch.

**Proposition (corrected).** Let ‖X‖_F, ‖Y‖_F ≤ r < (ln 2)/4 with X, Y ∈ 𝔞. Then

    dist(log(e^X e^Y), 𝔞) ≤ (δ/4) [ −ln(2 − e^{4r}) − 4r ]  =: δ φ(r),   φ(r) = 4r² + O(r³),

and, sharper in the leading term,

    dist(log(e^X e^Y), 𝔞) ≤ ½ δ r² + C(r₀) δ r³   for r ≤ r₀ < (ln 2)/4,   C(r₀) = (φ(r₀) − 4r₀²)/r₀³.

Both bounds are 0 under exact closure, consistent with Proposition 1.

**Proof.**
1. *Dynkin form.* Dynkin's formula writes log(e^X e^Y) = Σ_{m≥1} ((−1)^{m−1}/m) Σ [w]/(|w| Π r_i! s_i!).
   Here the inner sum runs over words w = X^{r_1}Y^{s_1}⋯X^{r_m}Y^{s_m} with r_i + s_i > 0, [w] is the right-nested
   commutator, and |w| = Σ(r_i + s_i).
2. *Norm of a nested commutator.* With the submultiplicative Frobenius norm, ‖[P, Q]‖ ≤ 2‖P‖‖Q‖, so
   ‖[w]‖ ≤ 2^{n−1} r^n for |w| = n.
3. *Escape of a nested commutator.* Let e_n bound ‖N[w]‖ for |w| = n. Then e_1 = 0 and e_2 ≤ δr².
   Write [w] = [W, Z] with W ∈ {X, Y} and Z the length-(n−1) commutator, and split Z = P₀Z + NZ:
   * [W, P₀Z] has both arguments in 𝔞, so its escape is at most δ r ‖Z‖ ≤ δ 2^{n−2} r^n;
   * ‖[W, NZ]‖ ≤ 2r e_{n−1}.

   Induction gives e_n ≤ (n−1) 2^{n−2} δ r^n.
4. *Summation.* Each word contributes at most ((n−1)/n) 2^{n−2} δ r^n / Π r_i! s_i! ≤ (δ/4)(2r)^n / Π r_i! s_i!.
   * Summing over all tuples gives Σ_m (1/m)(e^{4r} − 1)^m = −ln(2 − e^{4r}), finite for r < (ln 2)/4.
   * Subtracting the length-1 words (exactly in 𝔞; their bound is 4r) gives the first bound.
   * The length-2 terms are exactly ½[X, Y], whose escape is at most ½δr², while their contribution to the series
     bound is 16r²·(δ/4) = 4δr². Replacing that contribution gives the second bound.
   * Because −ln(2 − e^u) − u has non-negative Taylor coefficients, (φ(r) − 4r²)/r³ is increasing, so C(r₀) bounds
     it on [0, r₀] (`test_T5_series_coefficients_nonnegative`).
5. *Equality with the principal logarithm.* The series converges absolutely on the ball r < (ln 2)/4, and
   ‖e^X e^Y − I‖ ≤ e^{2r} − 1 < 1 there. Both sides are analytic in (X, Y) on this connected ball and agree near 0
   (Hall 2015, Thm 5.3), so they agree on the whole ball. ∎

Tests:
* `test_T5_prop2_rigorous_bound`: 400 random pairs over four (perturbation, radius) settings, against both bounds.
* `test_T5_exact_closure_zero_escape`.

**Combining with Lemma 1** gives a bound on the escape purely in terms of the loss:
dist ≤ K √L_close φ(r) / λ_min(G). For the paper's coefficient bound 0.65 and ‖A_k‖ = √2, single-edge actions have
‖A(a)‖_F ≤ 0.65 √(2K). This exceeds (ln 2)/4 ≈ 0.173, so **the bound does not cover every action the model can
infer**. It applies to sufficiently small coefficients only, and the manuscript says so.

## 6. Proposition 3 (chained orthogonal stability): **correct**

If d_t = ẑ_t − z_t, then d_{t+1} = R_t d_t − e_t, and ‖R_t d_t‖ = ‖d_t‖. Iterating gives
‖ẑ_T − z_T‖ ≤ ‖ẑ_0 − z_0‖ + Σ_{t<T} ‖e_t‖. Test: `test_prop3_chained_stability`. In our setting the bound applies
to latent transport. Decoding adds the decoder's Lipschitz constant, which the manuscript does not bound.

## 7. Projected Jacobi residual: **clarified**

For matrix commutators the Jacobi identity holds identically. Only the bracket projected onto 𝔞, with structure
constants c^k_ij = (G⁻¹Bᵀ vec[A_i, A_j])_k, can violate it, and its Jacobi residual is what `jacobi_residual`
reports. It is a diagnostic only.

## 8. What closure does and does not guarantee

Four distinct properties, each implied by the next but not conversely:

1. **Algebraic closure.** 𝔞 is a subalgebra (L_close = 0).
2. **Correct isomorphism and conjugacy class.** 𝔞 is conjugate in SO(d) or O(d) to the true algebra 𝔤 ⊂ so(d).
3. **Ground-truth recovery in the learned coordinates.** After the latent alignment determined by the data (the
   orthogonal Procrustes map from E(x) to the true state), span(R𝔞Rᵀ) = 𝔤. This is what `gt_algebra_distance` measures.
4. **Representation equivalence with correct action inference.** Additionally, ρ(q(E x, E x')) ≈ R⁻¹ g R on data,
   measured by `action_disagreement_truth`.

**Classification results used in the failure analysis** (standard facts, with numerical checks in T6):
* **T² (K = 2) in so(5).** Every subalgebra of so(d) is compact, hence reductive, and a 2-dimensional reductive Lie
  algebra is abelian. A closed, non-collapsed 2-dimensional 𝔞 is therefore a maximal abelian subalgebra, since
  rank so(5) = 2, hence a Cartan subalgebra. All Cartan subalgebras of a compact Lie algebra are conjugate
  (maximal-torus theorem). So, for T², 1 ⇒ 2. Failures of 3 can only come from the latent coordinates.
* **SO(3) (K = 3) in so(4) ≅ su(2) ⊕ su(2).** A 3-dimensional subalgebra 𝔥 is reductive. It cannot be abelian
  (rank 2), and no 3-dimensional reductive algebra mixes u(1) with a 2-dimensional piece, so 𝔥 ≅ su(2). Each
  projection onto a factor is either zero or an isomorphism:
  * one projection zero: 𝔥 is a **chiral factor** su(2)_L or su(2)_R; the two are exchanged by a reflection of O(4);
  * both isomorphisms: 𝔥 is the graph of an automorphism, which is inner for su(2), so 𝔥 is conjugate to the
    **diagonal** so(3) acting on R³ ⊕ R. This is the true action.

  The two classes are told apart by the conjugation-invariant participation ratio of unit elements (2 for diagonal,
  4 for chiral) and by κ (√2 vs 2). So for SO(3), 1 ⇏ 2: **a closed but incorrect algebra is possible**, and it was
  observed experimentally.
* **Counterexample to the "commutator norm identifies the algebra" reading.** Diagonal and chiral su(2) both close
  exactly, yet their mean commutator norms are √2 and 2. Test: `test_commutator_norm_does_not_identify_representation`.
* **No identifiability theorem is claimed.** With unlabeled transitions, the objectives are unchanged by
  (i) GL(K) reparameterization of the generator coordinates (up to the normalization), (ii) conjugation of the
  latent space by O(d), and (iii) any reparameterization of the encoder that commutes with the learned action.
  Nothing in the objective forces property 3 or 4.
